# 研究内容一E1正式实验报告V1.0

## 第一部分 实验目标与研究问题

E1 evaluates representation size, compilation cost, matching cost, input
redundancy invariance, and the degradation boundary of normalized intervals
and dyadic cover. Evidence: Figures 4-2--4-5; Tables 4-2 and 4-4; processed
CSVs in `processed/`; raw data in `raw/results.csv`.

`False`. Python is 3.13.11;

## 第三部分 数据生成方法

The formal matrix contains 168 deterministic samples: 108 E1-A main-effect,
36 E1-B redundancy, and 24 E1-C boundary samples. Every dataset row records
target and realized coverage, fragmentation, redundancy, raw policy,
normalized policy, seed, and configuration hash. Evidence:
`datasets/e1_samples.jsonl` and copied configuration under `configs/`.

## 第四部分 基线和指标

Slot enumeration uses a frozen set and uint64 logical slots; normalized
intervals use the same I* for all methods, fixed-width endpoint pairs, and
binary search; dyadic cover uses fixed-width `(start,size)` nodes and its
frozen matcher. Logical bytes, deep resident bytes, peak allocation,
construction stages, batched query latency, and throughput remain distinct.
Evidence: Table 4-2 and `processed/e1_statistical_summary.csv`.

## 第五部分 E1-A主效应结果

Against slot enumeration, dyadic cover is smaller in 36/108
main-effect samples, concentrated in continuous/high-coverage policies.
Against normalized intervals it is smaller in 0/108,
equal in 36/108, and larger in 72/108.
Thus dyadic cover is not a general one-dimensional compression of I*.
Evidence: Figures 4-2 and 4-5, Table 4-4,
`processed/e1_representation_ratios.csv`, and `raw/results.csv`.

Median-of-sample-median query costs are approximately
350.4 ns (enumeration),
561.0 ns (interval), and
1984.7 ns (dyadic). These are Python implementation
constants, not a proof of asymptotic superiority. Evidence: Figure 4-4 and
`processed/figure_4_4_data.csv`.

## 第六部分 E1-B输入冗余结果

The E1-B matrix keeps semantic generation deterministic for each seed and
fragmentation while varying R in {1,2,4,8}. Normalized policies, canonical
encoding, and digest are invariant for semantically equivalent redundant
inputs; normalization time still includes the additional raw intervals.
Evidence: E1-B rows in `datasets/e1_samples.jsonl`,
`processed/e1_statistical_summary.csv`, and `raw/results.csv`.

## 第七部分 E1-C边界与退化结果

Aligned single intervals require one interval and one cover node. The
non-aligned near-full interval requires 25 cover nodes for one interval.
Even, odd, random-isolated, and maximum-fragmentation policies reach
`c=k`, while a non-power-of-two full domain uses six cover nodes for one
interval. These results expose both alignment benefits and fragmentation
degradation. Evidence: Table 4-4, Figure 4-5, and E1-C rows in
`processed/e1_representation_ratios.csv`.

## 第八部分 统计分析

Each sample-method metric reports n, mean, median, standard deviation, minimum,
maximum, p95, and deterministic 95% percentile-bootstrap intervals. Compilation
median trends by U are {1000: 0.07435, 10000: 0.5338, 100000: 5.7972, 1000000: 123.2306} ms. The measurements are consistent with
output-sensitive growth but do not prove `O(n log n + c)`. No post-hoc outlier
deletion was applied. Evidence: `processed/e1_statistical_summary.csv`.

## 第十部分 主要发现

Canonical normalization removes ordering, duplication, and equivalent-split
differences. Interval and dyadic representations substantially beat slot
enumeration only when continuity is sufficient. Dyadic node count grows with
fragmentation and cannot be claimed to compress arbitrary discontinuous
policies logarithmically. Its defensible role is a canonical hierarchical
authorization interface for later research, not universal storage or lookup
superiority. Evidence: Figures 4-2--4-5 and Tables 4-2--4-4.

## 第十一部分 负面结果与局限

Normalized intervals are never larger than the dyadic cover under the common
16-byte fixed-width encoding in E1-A. Enumeration is fastest for Python
membership queries in the measured matrix, at the cost of O(A) space.
Scheduling was parallelized only after a serial throughput bottleneck was
observed; sample definitions, warmups, repeats, queries, methods, and metrics
were unchanged. Machine-level timing remains subject to ordinary OS noise.
